TITLE: Isofetamid efficacy against SDHI-resistant fungal pathogens
URL: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC13013558/

Present address: Kagawa Prefectural Agricultural Experiment Station, Kagawa 760–8570, Japan
Present address: Nihon Nohyaku Co., Ltd., Tokyo 104–8386, Japan
In this study, we report the results of sensitivity tests to succinate dehydrogenase inhibitor (SDHI) fungicides performed on isolates of cucumber Corynespora leaf spot, powdery mildew, and tomato leaf mold pathogens collected in Japan. Isofetamid and fluopyram showed strong activity against boscalid-resistant isolates of Corynespora cassiicola from Tokushima Prefecture in in vitro antifungal assays and in vivo pot experiments. Isofetamid and fluopyram also showed comparable efficacy against boscalid- and penthiopyrad-resistant isolate of Podosphaera xanthii collected in Saga Prefecture using a leaf disc assay. No change was detected in the amino acid sequence of sdhB in the causal pathogen. In addition, during in vitro antifungal tests, isofetamid demonstrated high activity against Fulvia fulva isolates resistant to boscalid, penthiopyrad, and fluopyram obtained in Gifu Prefecture. Collectively, these results indicated that isofetamid maintained similar efficacy against isolates resistant to other SDHIs as against sensitive isolates of these pathogens.
Succinate dehydrogenase inhibitors (SDHIs) are important broad-spectrum fungicides widely used in agriculture. After the introduction of SDHIs, such as boscalid and penthiopyrad, that are effective against ascomycetes, several new fungicidal compounds have been developed. These fungicides are now widely applied as foliage sprays and seed treatments across a range of crops, including cereals, fruits, vegetables, turf, and ornamental plants. 1 – 4 ) Therefore, this fungicide family is a key component of disease control strategies, alongside demethylation inhibitors (DMIs) and quinone outside inhibitors (Q o Is). 4 – 8 ) However, isolates resistant to SDHIs have been reported in numerous plant pathogens since the introduction of boscalid and penthiopyrad to the market. 1 , 8 – 10 ) Recently, resistant isolates of Alternaria alternata , A. brassicicola, Botrytis cinerera , B. elliptica, Corynespora cassicola , Didymella bryoniae , Fulvia fulva , Podosphaera xanthii , Sclerotinia sclerotiorum , and Stemphylium botryosum have been reported in various countries. 1 , 8 – 20 )
Isofetamid, chemically known as N -[1,1-dimethyl-2-(4-isopropoy- o -tolyl)-2-oxoethyl]-3-methylthiophene-2-carboxamide (code name: IKF-5411; trade names: KENJA®, ZENBY®, KRYOR®, HAREGI®, KABUTO®, and ASTUN®), is a new fungicide developed by Ishihara Sangyo Kaisha, Ltd. 9 , 10 , 21 – 23 ) We previously reported that isofetamid belongs to the SDHI class (FRAC code 7). It specifically inhibits succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) activity in ascomycetes but does not affect SDH activity in oomycetes, plants, or mammals. 21 ) We also described the fungicidal spectrum of isofetamid and its impact on infection processes of B. cinerea . 21 ) Furthermore, its biological properties have been demonstrated in greenhouse pot experiments, and its practical performance has been validated in field trials. 22 ) Isofetamid has also been shown not to exhibit clear cross-resistance with existing SDHIs against gray mold fungus. 23 )
Although preliminary studies suggested that isofetamid may be effective against plant pathogens that are resistant to other SDHI fungicides, its detailed biological properties remain incompletely investigated. 9 , 10 ) In this study, we examined the efficacy of isofetamid against SDHI-resistant isolates of cucumber Corynespora leaf spot, powdery mildew, and tomato leaf mold pathogens. Based on our findings, we discussed the unique properties of isofetamid in disease management compared to other SDHI fungicides.
Tested fungal isolates of three pathogens ( C. cassiicola , P. xanthii , and F. fulva ) were listed in Supplemental Table S1. A suspension concentrate (SC) of isofetamid (400 g/L) and a 50.0% dry flowable formulation of boscalid (Cantus®, BASF Japan, Ltd.) were used for tests against three pathogens. Fluopyram 500 g/L SC (Alfine®, Bayer CropScience) was used for tests against Corenespora leaf spot pathogen, whereas fluopyram 500 g/L SC (Luna Privilege®, Bayer CropScience) was used for tests against cucumber powdery mildew and tomato leaf mold pathogens. Penthiopyrad 20.0% SC (Aphet®, Mitsui Chemicals Crop & Life Solutions, Inc.) was used for tests against cucumber powdery mildew and tomato leaf spot pagthogens (Supplemental Table S1).
Mycelia from each pre-cultured isolate of C. cassiicola collected from cucumber fields in Tokushima Prefecture, grown on potato sucrose agar medium were excised using a cork borer (5 mm diameter). Each mycelial disc was transferred to yeast bacto acetate (YBA) agar medium supplemented with isofetamid, boscalid or fluopyram (100, 10, 1, 0.1, 0.01 µg/mL, n =3) and then incubated at 20°C for 6 days. After incubation, the diameters of the resulting colonies were measured to estimate EC 50 values using Probit method. The experiments were repeated 2 times, showing the similar results.
Next, the preventive efficacy of isofetamid against these isolates was tested on cucumber seedlings in pot experiments. Cucumber seedlings ( Cucumis sativus , cv. Natsusuzumi) were grown in a greenhouse, and the adaxial surface of the leaves were sprayed with isofetamid, boscalid or fluopyram solutions using a handgun sprayer (ANEST IWATA RG-3L, 40 mL/0.2 m 2 ; equivalent to 2,000 L/ha) at two-leaf stage. After drying, the seedlings were inoculated with aqueous spore suspensions of C. cassiicola (1.0–2.0×10 5 conidia/mL) by the same type of sprayer. The inoculated plants were then incubated at 20°C for 7–11 days. Fungicidal activity was assessed by visually assessing the lesion area of the first leaf and expressed as disease severity (0–100%). 22 ) Control values were calculated using the following equations: Control value=(1 – T / C )×100, where T is the disease severity of treated plants and C is the disease severity of the untreated plants. For statistical analysis, relative rates (ranging from 0 to 1) were calculated based on these control values. Each value underwent arcsine transformation to stabilize variance. We conducted a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by Tukey’s post hoc honestly significant difference (HSD) test, utilizing the multiple-sample comparison tool available at http://astatsa.com/OneWay_Anova_with_TukeyHSD/. Statistical significance was determined at p <0.01. The averages of 3 independent control values for each treatment group were shown in the graphs. The experiments were repeated 2 times, showing the similar results.
A slightly modified leaf disc assay 10 , 24 ) was performed on a boscalid-sensitive isolate (PX-1) from our laboratory and a boscalid-resistant isolate (PX-2) of cucumber powdery mildew pathogen P. xanthii , collected from Saga Prefecture. Cucumber seedlings ( C. sativus , cv. Sagami hanjiro) were grown in a greenhouse, and the adaxial surface of the leaves were sprayed with isofetamid, boscalid, penthiopyrad or fluopyram solutions (333, 100, 33, 10, 3, 1, 0.3, 0.1 µg/mL) using a method described in section 2. After drying, leaf discs (8 mm diameter) were cut from the first leaves and placed on Petri dishes containing 0.2% water agar. These discs were inoculated by dusting conidia of P. xanthii . The inoculated discs ( n =8) were incubated at 20°C for 10 days under continuous light (fluorescent light, 2,200 lx). Fungicidal activity was evaluated by visually assessing the lesion area and expressed as the disease suppression rates ranging from 0 to 100%. The minimum effective concentration (MEC) represented the minimum concentration achieving >90% suppression rates. This test was conducted one time.
The sequence of the sdhB gene in this resistant isolate (PX-2) was examined. Genomic DNA was extracted from the resistant isolate (PX-2) using the DNeasy plant mini kit (QIAGEN) according to the manufactured protocol. DNA fragments containing the sdhB gene were amplified using sdhB -specific primers (Forward: 5′-TGYCCVTCBTACTGGTGGAA-3′, Reverse: 5′-GGRCAKGYYCKNGWRCARTT-3′). The amplified PCR products were sequenced using a specific primer (5′-TTCTGAGGAGTATCTTGGCC-3′) and the BigDye Terminator Version 3.1 (Applied Biosystems). The PCR and sequencing conditions followed those described in a previous paper, which was reported by Miyamoto et al. 15 , 20 ) The obtained sequences were compared with reference sequence available in the NCBI database (Accession number AB54741).
YBA agar medium containing isofetamid, boscalid, penthiopyrad, and fluopyram was dispensed into 48-well microplates. Mycelia of pre-cultured isolates of the tomato leaf mold pathogen F. fulva collected from Gifu Prefecture were excised using a cork borer (5 mm in diameter) and homogenized with 1 mL sterile water in a 1.5 mL sterilized microtube using a pestle. Subsequently, 20 µL of homogenate was transferred onto each test medium containing each SDHI (125, 25, 5, 1, 0.2, 0.04, 0.008 µg/mL, n =3) and incubated at 25°C for 10 days. After incubation, fungicidal activity was evaluated by visually assessing the appearance of colonies, and the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) was determined. 10 , 17 ) The experiments were repeated 3 times, showing the similar results.
The activities of isofetamid and other SDHIs against C. cassiicola isolates collected from cucumber fields in Tokushima Prefecture was tested in both in vitro antifungal assays and in vivo pot experiments. For boscalid, we obtained EC 50 values of isolates 262, 268, 272, and 276 that ranged from 0.12 to 0.25 µg/mL, resulting in their classification as “sensitive”. In contrast, the EC 50 values of isolates 11, 12, 181, and 182 were >10 µg/mL, identifying them as “highly resistant” ( Table 1 ). Mycelial growth of all eight isolates was severely inhibited in the presence of isofetamid and fluopyram, with EC 50 values ranging from 0.06 to 0.65 µg/mL and 0.19 to 0.78 µg/mL, respectively ( Table 1 ). This indicated that isofetamid and fluopyram displayed similar efficacy against both boscalid-sensitive and boscalid-resistant isolates of C. cassiicola .
Mycelial growth was measured in YBA agar medium amended with isofetamid, boscalid, and fluopyram and EC 50 values were obtained.
Next, the preventive efficacies of SDHIs against these isolates was tested on cucumber seedlings in pot experiments. Boscalid showed good efficacy against isolates 268, 272, and 276 at 25 µg/mL but failed to control isolates 11 and 12, even at a practical concentration (333 µg/mL). Therefore, isolates 11 and 12 were confirmed as highly resistant, with control failures likely in the field ( Fig. 1 ). In contrast, isofetamid was highly effective against all five tested isolates at a practical concentration (266 µg/mL). Furthermore, it achieved a control value above 80% even at a low concentration of 25 µg/mL ( Fig. 1 ). Fluopyram was also effective against all five isolates as isofetamid at 25 µg/mL. Although statistically significant differences in the control values of isofetamid and fluopyram were detected among the isolates, the variation was minimal and thus considered biologically insignificant ( Fig. 1 ). These results confirmed that isofetamid and fluopyram exhibited similar efficacy against boscalid-resistant isolates of C. cassiicola ( Table 1 and Fig. 1 ).
Differences in cross-resistance among SDHIs have also been observed in B. cinerea 13 , 14 , 23 ) infecting several important crops and in C. cassiicoila 10 , 15 , 16 ) on cucumber. The H272Y and H272R mutations in the sdhB gene of B. cinerea did not reduce the sensitivity to isofetamid and fluopyram. 13 , 14 , 23 ) Interestingly, in C. cassiicola , the H278Y mutation in sdhB which corresponds to H272Y in B. cinerea , lowered sensitivity to boscalid but did not lower sensitivity to isofetamid 10 ) or fluopyram. 15 , 16 )
Sensitivity tests for SDHIs against cucumber powdery mildew pathogen P. xanthii isolate collected from Saga Prefecture were carried out using leaf disc assay. The MEC value of isolate PX-1 to boscalid was 10 µg/mL, whereas that of isolate PX-2 was >333 µg/mL, indicating that PX-1 was sensitive and PX-2 was highly resistant to boscalid. Penthiopyrad also showed little activity against isolate PX-2, indicating that PX-2 is resistant to both boscalid and penthiopyrad. However, isofetamid and fluopyram showed similar efficacy against isolate PX-2 as they did against the sensitive isolate PX-1 ( Table 2 ). Thus, these fungicides did not exhibit cross-resistance with boscalid or penthiopyrad in this resistant P. xanthii isolate. Moreover, previous studies on cucumber powdery mildew have reported that fluopyram lacks cross-resistance to both boscalid and penthiopyrad 16 ) and that isofetamid lacks cross resistance to penthiopyrad. 19 )
Leaf disc assay was conducted against two isolates of P. xanthii for 4 SDHIs.
The sequence of the sdhB gene in this resistant isolate was examined. DNA fragments containing the sdhB gene were amplified from the DNA fraction extracted from the resistant isolate using sdhB -specific primers. Obtained sequences were analyzed and compared with those of sensitive isolate. No mutation was detected in the sdhB gene of isolate PX-2, confirming the same amino acid sequence as in the previously reported sensitive isolate (data not shown). 20 ) Possible amino acid changes in sdhC and adhD subunits remain unknown in PX-2. Recently, Miyamoto et al. have identified several mutations in the sdhC and sdhD genes of SDHI-resitant P. xanthii isolates obtained from Ibaraki Prefecture, although no sdhB mutation was found. 19 ) Furthermore, they did not report the effect of fluopyram against these SDHI-resistant isolates. 19 )
Subsequently, sensitivity assays were conducted against isolates of the tomato leaf mold fungus F. fulva collected from Gifu Prefecture using mycelial growth inhibition tests. 17 ) Isolates PF-1 and PF-2 were sensitive to all tested SDHIs, with MIC values <0.2 µg/mL. In contrast, PF-3 and PF-7 showed MICs >125 µg/mL for boscalid and penthiopyrad, indicating that these isolates were highly resistant to these fungicides. Moreover, the MIC value of PF-3 for fluopyram was 5 µg/mL, whereas that of PF-7 was >125 µg/mL. Therefore, PF-3 showed moderate resistance to fluopyram, while PF-7 showed high resistance ( Table 3 ). The MIC values of isolates PF-5 and PF-6 to boscalid were >125 µg/mL, whereas those for penthiopyrad and fluopyram were 0.2 µg/mL. These isolates were resistant to boscalid and sensitive to both penthiopyrad and fluopyram.
Mycelial growth inhibition tests were conducted against 6 isolates of F. fulva for 4 SDHIs.
Isofetamid maintained strong antifungal activity against all four resistant isolates, with MIC values of 0.04–0.2 µg/mL. These findings confirmed that isofetamid showed similar efficacy against isolates resistant to other SDHIs as it did against sensitive isolates ( Table 3 ). Notably, it did not exhibit cross-resistance with boscalid, penthiopyrad, or fluopyram in isolates PF-3 and PF-7 of F. fluva . Cross resistance relationships in this fungus appear complex as suggested by other recent studies. 17 , 18 ) Asao et al. reported different levels of sensitivity reduction among SDHI fungicides and demonstrated that isofetamid showed the lowest potential for resistance among the tested SDHI fungicides. 17 ) On the other hand, Hirai et al. reported that boscalid-resistant isolates were all cross resistant to penthiopyrad, fluopyram, and isofetamid, although the degree of cross resistance differed considerably. 18 ) Furthermore, all these isolates had mutations on sdhC genes. 18 ) Taken together, our findings show the same trend, though they were based on a limited number of isolates and tested SDHI fungicides.
In this study, sensitivity assays were conducted with cucumber Corynespora leaf spot, powdery mildew, and tomato leaf mold pathogens collected from Tokushima, Saga, and Gifu Prefectures, respectively. These isolates showed reduced sensitivity to boscalid, penthiopyrad and/or fluopyram; however, isofetamid retained similar efficacy against resistant and sensitive isolates ( Tables 1 – 3 and Fig. 1 ). These findings demonstrate that isofetamid is highly effective against SDHI-resistant isolates, which have become increasingly common in recent years and pose serious challenges in major crops. This highlights the advantageous position of isofetamid among SDHI fungicides.
Furthermore, our earlier report showed that isofetamid displayed reduced sensitivity against B. cinerea isolates carrying H272L and H225F mutations in sdhB. 23 ) Competition experiments between isolates with H272L or H225F and the sensitive isolates revealed that the resistant isolates gradually disappeared after several generations. 23 ) These observations suggest that the low detection frequency of such mutants in the field 13 , 14 ) may be related to the fitness costs associated with these isolates. These findings further indicate the distinctive role of isofetamid in disease management compared with other SDHI fungicides.
Finally, the presence or absence of cross-resistance between isofetamid and other SDHIs also remains noteworthy. Overall, the results of this study, along with published reports on the efficacy of SDHI fungicides against various plant pathogens, do not support the classical concept of positive cross-resistance among chemicals sharing a single mode of action. Instead, some mutations sharply reduce sensitivity to specific SDHI fungicides while exerting only minimal effects on others. 8 , 11 – 14 , 16 – 19 , 23 )
